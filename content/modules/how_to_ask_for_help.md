# How to Ask for Help

## Goal

Learn how to ask a mentor, IT tech, or supervisor for help clearly and confidently when you are stuck, unsure, or facing a task that may not be beginner-safe.

Asking for help is not a weakness. In IT, asking at the right time prevents mistakes and protects users, devices, data, and access.

---

## Plain-Language Explanation

Asking for help means clearly explaining what is happening, what you already checked, and what you need next.

In simple terms:

> A good help request gives your mentor enough context to guide you.

You do not need to know the answer before asking. You only need to describe the situation honestly and avoid guessing.

---

## Why This Matters

IT work often involves systems that affect other people.

If you guess, you might:

- Change the wrong thing.
- Work on the wrong device.
- Miss an important error.
- Affect a user's data or access.
- Create extra work for the team.

If you ask clearly, your mentor can help faster and teach you what to look for next time.

---

## Key Terms

### Mentor

A mentor is a person assigned to help you learn the role, understand workflows, and make safe decisions.

---

### Context

Context is the background information that helps someone understand the situation.

Examples:

- Which device is affected.
- What the user was trying to do.
- What error appeared.
- What step you were on.
- What you already checked.

---

### Symptom

A symptom is what the user or device is experiencing.

Examples:

- Monitor shows No Signal.
- Keyboard is not responding.
- Login gives an error.
- Computer is running slowly.

---

### Error Message

An error message is text, a number, or a code that appears when something goes wrong.

Write it down exactly. Do not summarize it if you can capture the exact wording.

---

### Escalation

Escalation means asking someone with more experience, access, or authority to help.

Escalation is part of safe IT work.

---

### Next Step

The next step is what should happen after the current check or task.

If you do not know the next step, ask before continuing.

---

## What You Need to Know

As a new IT work-study student, you should understand these basic ideas:

1. You are expected to ask questions.
2. Clear context helps mentors help you faster.
3. It is better to say "I am not sure" than to guess.
4. Exact error messages matter.
5. Do not hide mistakes or uncertainty.
6. Asking early can prevent bigger problems.
7. A good help request includes the issue, checks, result, and question.

---

## Beginner-Safe Things You Can Do

Depending on department procedure, beginner-safe actions may include:

- Pause before continuing when you are unsure.
- Write down the exact error message.
- Record the device name or asset tag.
- Note what step you were on.
- Summarize what you checked.
- Ask your mentor what the next approved step is.
- Prepare a short mentor message or draft summary.

These habits make escalation easier and safer.

---

## What You Should Not Do Without Approval

Do not do these when you are stuck:

- Guess which workflow applies.
- Keep clicking through unfamiliar prompts.
- Restart a process repeatedly without telling anyone.
- Hide an error or mistake.
- Tell a user something is fixed if you are unsure.
- Use admin credentials.
- Change settings just to see what happens.
- Update or close a ticket before you are trained and authorized.

When in doubt, stop and ask.

---

## Common Beginner Mistakes

New work-study students may accidentally:

- Wait too long to ask for help.
- Say "it does not work" without explaining the symptom.
- Forget to include the error message.
- Leave out the asset tag or location.
- Say they tried "everything" instead of listing what they checked.
- Feel embarrassed asking basic questions.
- Guess because they want to seem independent.

Good IT work is careful, not performative.

---

## When to Ask for Help

Ask your mentor, IT tech, or supervisor when:

- You are unsure what to do next.
- The issue involves accounts, permissions, MFA, imaging, or sensitive data.
- You see an unfamiliar error.
- A user is waiting and you do not know what to say.
- You are unsure whether a task is beginner-safe.
- You may have made a mistake.
- The device or ticket does not match what you expected.
- You feel tempted to guess.

---

## Example Student Questions

### "How should I ask if I am stuck?"

Use this pattern:

> I am working on ____. I saw ____. I checked ____. The result was ____. What should I do next?

---

### "What if I feel embarrassed?"

It is normal to feel that way when something is new. Asking early is part of learning the role and protecting the team from preventable mistakes.

---

### "What if I made a mistake?"

Tell your mentor right away. Include what happened, what changed, and what you have not touched since then.

---

## Quick Check

Answer these in your own words.

1. Why is asking for help important in IT?
2. What information should you include when asking for help?
3. Why should you write down exact error messages?
4. What should you say if you do not know the answer to a user question?
5. What should you do if you may have made a mistake?
6. Why is guessing risky?

---

## Strong Answers

1. Asking for help prevents mistakes and brings in someone with the right experience, access, or authority.
2. Include the issue, device or location, error message, what you checked, what happened, and what help you need.
3. Exact error messages help mentors identify the issue and avoid confusion.
4. Say you are going to check with a mentor or IT staff member, then ask for help.
5. Tell your mentor right away and explain what happened honestly.
6. Guessing can affect users, devices, data, access, or documentation.
