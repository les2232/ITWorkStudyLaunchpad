# Hardware Basics

## Goal

Understand basic computer hardware terms, beginner-safe checks, and when to ask for help before touching equipment or making changes.

You do not need to repair hardware on your own as a new IT work-study student. The goal is to recognize common devices, document issues clearly, and follow safe boundaries.

---

## Plain-Language Explanation

Hardware means the physical parts of technology.

In simple terms:

> Hardware is the equipment you can touch.

Examples include computers, monitors, keyboards, mice, cables, docking stations, printers, projectors, and power adapters.

Some hardware checks are beginner-safe when your department allows them. Other hardware work may require training, tools, approval, or a more experienced technician.

---

## Why This Matters

Many common IT requests involve hardware.

For example:

- A monitor says No Signal.
- A keyboard or mouse is not working.
- A laptop is not charging.
- A computer will not turn on.
- A projector is not displaying.
- A cable is loose or connected to the wrong port.

Knowing basic hardware terms helps you follow instructions, ask better questions, and document what you see.

---

## Key Terms

### Hardware

Hardware is the physical equipment used with technology.

Examples:

- Desktop computer
- Laptop
- Monitor
- Keyboard
- Mouse
- Printer
- Projector
- Docking station
- Cables

---

### Desktop Computer

A desktop computer is usually a computer that stays at a desk or workstation.

It may connect to a monitor, keyboard, mouse, network cable, and power cable.

---

### Laptop

A laptop is a portable computer with a built-in screen, keyboard, battery, and trackpad.

Laptops may also use a docking station to connect to monitors, keyboards, mice, network cables, or power.

---

### Monitor

A monitor is a screen connected to a computer or docking station.

Common beginner-safe checks may include confirming the monitor has power, is turned on, and is set to the correct input/source if instructed.

---

### Peripheral

A peripheral is an external device connected to a computer.

Examples:

- Keyboard
- Mouse
- Webcam
- Headset
- Printer
- Scanner

---

### Cable

A cable connects devices or provides power.

Common examples:

- Power cable
- HDMI
- DisplayPort
- USB
- USB-C
- Ethernet/network cable

Do not force a cable into a port. If it does not fit, stop and ask.

---

### Docking Station

A docking station, or dock, lets a laptop connect to multiple devices at once.

A dock may connect to monitors, keyboard, mouse, network, power, and USB devices.

---

### Monitor Input or Source

The monitor input/source controls which connection the monitor is showing.

Examples:

- HDMI
- DisplayPort
- USB-C
- VGA

If the monitor is on the wrong input, it may show No Signal.

---

### Asset Tag

An asset tag is a label or ID used to track equipment.

Do not remove, change, or guess asset tag information.

---

## What You Need to Know

As a new IT work-study student, you should understand these basic ideas:

1. Hardware means physical technology equipment.
2. Many hardware issues start with simple checks.
3. Beginner-safe checks depend on department procedure.
4. Do not force cables, open equipment, or use tools without approval.
5. Document exactly what you see.
6. Asset tags help the department track equipment.
7. Ask for help when the issue is unfamiliar or the next step is unclear.

---

## Beginner-Safe Things You Can Do

Depending on department procedure, beginner-safe actions may include:

- Confirm a device has power.
- Confirm a monitor is turned on.
- Check whether a cable is visibly connected.
- Check monitor input/source if instructed.
- Try a known-working keyboard or mouse if allowed.
- Record the asset tag.
- Write down the exact error message.
- Tell your mentor what you checked and what happened.

These checks gather useful information without opening equipment or changing restricted settings.

---

## What You Should Not Do Without Approval

Do not do these unless you are trained and authorized:

- Open a computer case.
- Remove internal parts.
- Force a cable or connector.
- Use tools on equipment.
- Replace parts without approval.
- Move equipment without instruction.
- Remove or change asset tags.
- Delete user data while troubleshooting.
- Tell a user the issue is fixed if you are unsure.

When in doubt, stop and ask.

---

## Common Beginner Mistakes

New work-study students may accidentally:

- Forget to check power first.
- Miss a loose cable.
- Use the wrong monitor input/source.
- Force the wrong cable into a port.
- Move equipment without checking asset tags.
- Assume a device is broken before testing simple items.
- Skip documenting what happened.
- Keep troubleshooting after the task stops being beginner-safe.

Simple checks matter, but safety boundaries matter too.

---

## When to Ask for Help

Ask your mentor, IT tech, or supervisor when:

- A device will not power on after basic checks.
- A cable or port looks damaged.
- A monitor still shows No Signal after approved checks.
- You are unsure which cable or port to use.
- Equipment needs to be opened or repaired.
- A device may contain user data.
- You are unsure whether you are allowed to move or swap equipment.
- You feel tempted to guess.

---

## Example Student Questions

### "What should I check first if a monitor says No Signal?"

If your department allows it, check whether the monitor has power, the computer is on, the display cable is connected, and the monitor input/source is correct.

---

### "Can I open the computer to check inside?"

Not unless you are trained and authorized. Ask your mentor before opening equipment or using tools.

---

### "Why does the asset tag matter?"

The asset tag helps IT identify and track the correct device. It can prevent work from being done on the wrong equipment.
