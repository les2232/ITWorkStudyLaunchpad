# Ticket Basics

## Goal

Understand what tickets are, why IT teams use them, and how new work-study students can prepare clear draft notes or mentor summaries safely.

You may not be allowed to create, edit, close, or update tickets/work orders until you are trained and authorized. This module focuses on understanding tickets and practicing good documentation habits.

---

## Plain-Language Explanation

A ticket is a record of an issue, request, task, or support interaction.

In simple terms:

> A ticket helps IT track what happened, who needs help, and what still needs to be done.

Tickets keep work organized. They also help other IT staff understand the history of an issue if someone else needs to continue the work later.

---

## Why This Matters

Without good tickets or summaries, IT work can become confusing.

For example:

- A user may ask for an update.
- Another tech may need to continue the work.
- A mentor may need to know what you already checked.
- A supervisor may need to see whether an issue was resolved.
- The team may need a record of equipment movement or setup work.

Clear documentation saves time and prevents repeated work.

---

## Key Terms

### Ticket

A ticket is a record of an issue, request, task, or support interaction.

It may include who is affected, what the issue is, what has been checked, who is assigned, and whether more work is needed.

---

### Work Order

A work order is similar to a ticket. Some departments use the words differently, but both usually refer to tracked work that needs attention.

Follow your department's wording and workflow.

---

### Request

A request is something someone is asking IT to do.

Examples:

- Set up a computer.
- Install approved software.
- Move equipment.
- Prepare a classroom device.

---

### Incident

An incident is something that is broken, not working, or interrupting normal work.

Examples:

- A monitor has no signal.
- A user cannot log in.
- A printer is not printing.
- A computer shows an error.

---

### Ticket Notes

Ticket notes explain what happened while working on a ticket.

Good notes usually include:

- The issue or request.
- What was checked.
- What happened.
- What changed, if anything.
- What still needs to happen.
- Who was notified or asked for help.

---

### Draft Note

A draft note is a note you prepare for mentor review before it is added to a ticket/work-order system.

New students should use draft notes until they are trained and authorized to update the system directly.

---

### Status

Status describes where the work stands.

Examples:

- New
- In progress
- Waiting
- Escalated
- Resolved
- Closed

Do not change ticket status unless you are trained and approved.

---

### Escalation Summary

An escalation summary tells a mentor or IT tech what happened and what help is needed next.

It should be short, clear, and honest about what is known and unknown.

---

## What You Need to Know

As a new IT work-study student, you should understand these basic ideas:

1. Tickets help IT track work.
2. Good notes make it easier for others to continue the work.
3. Vague notes like "fixed" are usually not enough.
4. You should document facts, not guesses.
5. You should not mark work complete if you are unsure.
6. Draft notes and mentor summaries are useful practice.
7. Do not update tickets/work orders directly until trained and authorized.

---

## Beginner-Safe Things You Can Do

Depending on department procedure, beginner-safe actions may include:

- Read a ticket with your mentor.
- Identify what the user is asking for.
- Write down the exact error message.
- Record beginner-safe checks you were instructed to perform.
- Prepare a draft note for mentor review.
- Tell your mentor what still needs to happen.
- Ask whether the ticket needs an update.

These actions support documentation without giving you responsibility for restricted ticket actions.

---

## What You Should Not Do Without Approval

Do not do these unless you are trained and authorized:

- Create a ticket.
- Edit a ticket.
- Close a ticket.
- Change ticket priority or status.
- Assign a ticket to someone.
- Promise resolution in the ticket.
- Write guesses as facts.
- Include passwords or sensitive data in notes.

When in doubt, prepare a draft summary and ask your mentor.

---

## Common Beginner Mistakes

New work-study students may accidentally:

- Write notes that are too short.
- Say "fixed" without explaining what was checked.
- Leave out the exact error message.
- Forget to mention what still needs to happen.
- Mark something complete before a mentor confirms it.
- Include sensitive information.
- Update tickets before being approved.

Good documentation is a skill. It improves with practice.

---

## When to Ask for Help

Ask your mentor, IT tech, or supervisor when:

- You do not know whether you are allowed to update a ticket.
- You do not know what status applies.
- The issue involves accounts, permissions, data, or security.
- You are unsure whether the issue is resolved.
- The user asks for a timeline you cannot confirm.
- You need help writing a clear summary.
- You are tempted to close or update a ticket without approval.

---

## Example Student Questions

### "What should I put in my draft note?"

Include the issue, what you checked, what happened, and what still needs to be done.

Example:

> Checked monitor issue at front desk workstation. Confirmed monitor had power and DisplayPort cable was connected. Monitor still showed No Signal. Stopped and asked mentor for next step.

---

### "Can I close the ticket if the keyboard works now?"

Not unless you have been trained and authorized. Tell your mentor what happened and ask whether they want you to draft a note.

---

### "What should I leave out?"

Do not include passwords, private personal information, guesses, blame, or anything you are not sure is true.

## Quick Check

Answer these in your own words.

1. What is a ticket?
2. Why are ticket notes useful?
3. What should a good draft note include?
4. Why is "fixed" usually not enough?
5. What should you avoid putting in ticket notes?
6. What should you do before updating a ticket/work order directly?

---

## Strong Answers

1. A ticket is a record of an issue, request, task, or support interaction.
2. Ticket notes help the team understand what happened, what was checked, and what still needs to be done.
3. A good draft note includes the issue, checks performed, results, changes made, and next steps.
4. "Fixed" does not explain what happened or help another tech continue the work.
5. Avoid passwords, sensitive information, guesses, blame, and unconfirmed statements.
6. Wait until you are trained and authorized, and ask your mentor when unsure.
