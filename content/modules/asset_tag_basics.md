# Asset Tag Basics

## Goal

Understand what asset tags are, why IT departments use them, and how new work-study students can safely help document equipment information.

You do not need to manage the inventory system on your own. The goal is to recognize asset tags, avoid changing them without approval, and record information accurately when instructed.

---

## Plain-Language Explanation

An asset tag is a label or ID used to track equipment.

In simple terms:

> An asset tag helps IT know which device is which.

Asset tags may appear on computers, monitors, laptops, printers, projectors, docking stations, or other equipment.

---

## Why This Matters

IT departments support many devices. Without tracking, equipment can be misplaced, duplicated, assigned incorrectly, or worked on by mistake.

Asset tags help IT answer questions like:

- Which device is this?
- Where is it located?
- Who is using it?
- What status is it in?
- Has it been moved, replaced, repaired, or retired?
- Is this the correct device for the ticket or work order?

Accurate asset information prevents confusion and helps the team protect equipment.

---

## Key Terms

### Asset Tag

An asset tag is a label or ID used to track equipment.

It may be a sticker, barcode, number, QR code, or other identifier.

---

### Asset

An asset is equipment owned or tracked by the organization.

Examples:

- Desktop computer
- Laptop
- Monitor
- Printer
- Projector
- Docking station
- Tablet

---

### Inventory

Inventory is the system or process used to track equipment.

Inventory may include asset tag, serial number, location, assigned user, device name, status, and notes.

---

### Serial Number

A serial number is a manufacturer-provided identifier for a device.

It is different from an asset tag. The asset tag belongs to the organization's tracking process.

---

### Location

Location describes where equipment is assigned or physically located.

Examples:

- Front desk
- Lab room
- Classroom
- Workbench
- Storage area

---

### Device Name

A device name is the name assigned to a computer or other managed device.

It may be used in tickets, inventory, imaging, or management tools.

---

### Workbench

A workbench is an IT area where devices may be prepared, checked, repaired, imaged, or reviewed.

Moving a device to or from the workbench may require documentation.

---

## What You Need to Know

As a new IT work-study student, you should understand these basic ideas:

1. Asset tags help IT identify and track equipment.
2. Asset tags are not decoration and should not be removed.
3. The asset tag and serial number may both matter.
4. Equipment movement may need to be documented.
5. Accurate information helps prevent work on the wrong device.
6. You should not update inventory records until trained and authorized.
7. Ask when an asset tag is missing, unreadable, or does not match the task.

---

## Beginner-Safe Things You Can Do

Depending on department procedure, beginner-safe actions may include:

- Locate the asset tag on a device.
- Read the asset tag to your mentor.
- Write down the asset tag exactly as shown.
- Confirm the device location.
- Check whether the asset tag is damaged or unreadable.
- Tell your mentor if a device is missing a label.
- Prepare a draft note about equipment movement.

These actions help with tracking without changing inventory records directly.

---

## What You Should Not Do Without Approval

Do not do these unless you are trained and authorized:

- Remove an asset tag.
- Replace an asset tag.
- Write over or alter an asset tag.
- Guess an unreadable asset tag.
- Update inventory records directly.
- Move equipment without instruction.
- Mark equipment as missing, retired, or disposed.
- Work on a device if you are unsure it matches the ticket/work order.

When in doubt, stop and ask.

---

## Common Beginner Mistakes

New work-study students may accidentally:

- Mix up asset tag and serial number.
- Record the wrong number.
- Skip checking the asset tag before moving equipment.
- Assume two similar devices are interchangeable.
- Forget to report a missing or damaged tag.
- Update inventory before being trained.
- Work on the wrong device.

Careful identification is part of safe IT work.

---

## When to Ask for Help

Ask your mentor, IT tech, or supervisor when:

- The asset tag is missing.
- The asset tag is unreadable.
- The asset tag does not match the ticket/work order.
- You are unsure whether the device is the correct one.
- Equipment needs to be moved.
- Inventory may need to be updated.
- A device appears lost, damaged, or assigned to the wrong location.

---

## Example Student Questions

### "Is the asset tag the same as the serial number?"

No. A serial number usually comes from the manufacturer. An asset tag is used by the organization to track equipment.

---

### "What if the asset tag is missing?"

Do not guess. Tell your mentor or the IT tech on duty and document that the tag was missing if instructed.

---

### "Can I update the inventory record?"

Only if you have been trained and authorized. Otherwise, prepare the information for your mentor.
