# What Does IT Do?

## Goal

Understand what an IT department does, why people ask IT for help, and how a new IT work-study student fits into the team.

You do not need to know every system on your first day. The goal is to understand the purpose of IT support, the kinds of work you may see, and why safe boundaries matter.

---

## Plain-Language Explanation

IT stands for Information Technology.

In simple terms:

> IT helps people use technology safely and reliably.

In a school or workplace, IT may support computers, accounts, software, classrooms, networks, printers, phones, security tools, and other systems people need to do their work.

IT is not only "fixing computers." It is also planning, documenting, protecting data, setting up equipment, helping users, and making sure technology works for the organization.

---

## Why This Matters

New work-study students often see many different tasks and wonder how they fit together.

For example, IT staff may:

- Help a user who cannot log in.
- Prepare a computer for a new employee.
- Move equipment to a classroom.
- Check a printer issue.
- Image computers for a lab.
- Track equipment in inventory.
- Document work in a ticket.
- Escalate an issue to another team.

Understanding the big picture helps you ask better questions and avoid guessing when a task affects users, data, devices, or access.

---

## Key Terms

### IT

IT stands for Information Technology.

IT supports the technology systems people use for school, work, communication, learning, and operations.

---

### User

A user is the person using the technology or asking for help.

Examples:

- Student
- Faculty member
- Staff member
- Work-study student
- Visitor or guest

---

### Support

Support means helping users with technology issues, requests, questions, or setup needs.

Support work may include asking questions, checking beginner-safe items, documenting what happened, and escalating when needed.

---

### Ticket

A ticket is a record of an issue, request, task, or support interaction.

Tickets help IT track what needs to be done, who is working on it, and what happened.

New work-study students may practice draft ticket notes or mentor summaries before being approved to update the ticket/work-order system directly.

---

### Workflow

A workflow is the approved way a task is handled.

Examples:

- How a new computer is prepared.
- How a login issue is escalated.
- How equipment is moved.
- How ticket notes are reviewed.

Do not invent a workflow when you are unsure. Ask your mentor.

---

### Escalation

Escalation means asking someone with more experience, access, or authority to help.

Escalation is part of good IT work. It prevents mistakes and keeps users, devices, and data safer.

---

### Inventory

Inventory is the system or process used to track equipment.

IT may track device names, asset tags, serial numbers, locations, assigned users, and status.

---

## What You Need to Know

As a new IT work-study student, you should understand these basic ideas:

1. IT helps people use technology safely and reliably.
2. IT work includes support, setup, documentation, inventory, security, and escalation.
3. Many IT tasks have approved workflows.
4. Some tasks are beginner-safe, and some require training or authorization.
5. Good documentation helps the whole team.
6. It is better to ask early than to guess.
7. You are part of the team even while you are still learning.

---

## Beginner-Safe Things You Can Do

Depending on department procedure, beginner-safe actions may include:

- Greet a user and listen to the issue.
- Ask what the user was trying to do.
- Write down the exact error message.
- Check simple physical items if instructed, such as power, cables, or monitor input.
- Move equipment if instructed.
- Confirm an asset tag or location.
- Prepare a draft summary for your mentor.
- Ask which workflow applies.

These actions help the team without requiring restricted access or risky changes.

---

## What You Should Not Do Without Approval

Do not do these unless you are trained and authorized:

- Use admin credentials.
- Reset passwords.
- Change permissions.
- Delete user data.
- Choose imaging workflows.
- Promise a user that an issue is fixed when you are unsure.
- Create, edit, close, or update tickets/work orders before approval.
- Invent a procedure because you want to be helpful.

When in doubt, stop and ask.

---

## Common Beginner Mistakes

New work-study students may accidentally:

- Think they should already know everything.
- Guess instead of asking which workflow applies.
- Try to fix a problem outside their access level.
- Forget to document what happened.
- Promise a timeline before checking with a mentor.
- Feel embarrassed asking basic questions.
- Treat escalation like failure.

These mistakes are common, but they are preventable.

---

## When to Ask for Help

Ask your mentor, IT tech, or supervisor when:

- You do not know which workflow applies.
- The task involves accounts, permissions, passwords, or MFA.
- The task involves imaging, inventory, or sensitive data.
- A user is waiting and you do not know what to say.
- An error message is unfamiliar.
- You are unsure whether you are allowed to do something.
- You feel tempted to guess.

Asking early is a professional habit.

---

## Example Student Questions

### "Is IT just fixing computers?"

No. Fixing computers is one part of IT, but IT also supports accounts, software, networks, classrooms, security, documentation, inventory, and many other systems.

---

### "What if I do not know the answer?"

Say that you are going to check with your mentor or another IT staff member. Then document the question and ask for help.

---

### "How do I know what I am allowed to do?"

Ask your mentor which tasks are beginner-safe and which tasks require approval. When the answer is unclear, stop and ask before continuing.

---

## Quick Check

Answer these in your own words.

1. What does IT help people do?
2. Why are workflows important?
3. What is one beginner-safe way to help with a user issue?
4. Why is documentation important?
5. What should you do if you are unsure whether a task is allowed?
6. Why is escalation not failure?

---

## Strong Answers

1. IT helps people use technology safely and reliably.
2. Workflows help the team handle tasks consistently and avoid risky guesses.
3. A beginner-safe action is documenting the exact error message or checking cables if instructed.
4. Documentation helps other IT staff understand what happened, what was checked, and what still needs to be done.
5. Stop and ask your mentor, IT tech, or supervisor before continuing.
6. Escalation brings in someone with the right experience, access, or authority and helps prevent mistakes.
